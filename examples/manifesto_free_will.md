- **Proposition 7 (Coarse-Grained Reductionism & Micro-Causal Closure)**:
  Any macroscopic causal claim—including "downward causation," "teleonomy," and "constraint closure"—is a coarse-grained representation of micro-dynamics under specific boundary conditions. No macroscopic causal power exists that is not expressible as a function of Hamiltonian dynamics plus boundary conditions. The ontological distinctness of "life" is an effective-theory claim, not a claim about new physical primitives. Autopoiesis, constraint closure (Rosen, Mossio, Montvil), and organizational closure are formalizable as fixed-point mappings on phase-space trajectories; they introduce no new causal primitives.

- **Proposition 8 (Semantic Grounding as Physical Causal-Relational Mapping)**:
  Referential semantics is a physical relation between syntactic states and environmental configurations, realized through causal and functional coupling. Peircean triadic relations (sign-object-interpretant) are formal structures that physical systems can implement through causal embedding, not requiring biological substrates. Deep Thought's semantic underdetermination of "42" is a boundary condition problem: the referent "the Question" was never coupled as an input. Semantic grounding is therefore not an ontological primitive beyond formal description but a physical mapping resolvable within it.

- **Proposition 9 (Observer-Relativity Without First-Person Primitives)**:
  Quantum contextuality and relational quantum mechanics entail observer-relativity of measurement outcomes, but this observer-relativity is itself physically described by the universal wavefunction or path integral over the observer-system composite. Decoherence explains the emergence of apparently classical descriptions. Gödelian limits apply to formal axiomatic systems, not to physical processes; a physical theory can instantiate dynamics exceeding any single formal system's expressive power. "Participatory reality" is therefore compatible with and subsumed by physical reductionism; the observer is a physical subsystem, not an ontologically primitive first-person datum.

- **Proposition 10 (On the Irreversibility Concession)**:
  Proposition 1's restriction to time-reversible equations is partially retracted. Coarse-grained effective field theories and non-equilibrium thermodynamics describe genuinely irreversible macroscopic behavior while remaining grounded in reversible micro-dynamics plus coarse-graining operators and boundary conditions. Irreversibility is a real feature of physical description, not merely epistemological; it does not constitute a violation of micro-causal closure.

**Proposition 17 (Agency as Emergent Reason-Responsiveness):**
Let $A$ be an agent, $D$ a decision, and $R$ a set of reasons (beliefs, desires, values). Free will, properly formalized, is not an uncaused causal primitive but the property of $A$ wherein $D = f(R) + \epsilon$, where $f$ is a reason-responsive function mapping evaluative states to actions, and $\epsilon$ is noise bounded by the agent's cognitive architecture. Moral responsibility attaches to the $f$-component, not to the capacity to violate physical law. A system possesses free will iff it is reason-responsive; this property is realized by human neural dynamics and is fully compatible with micro-physical causal closure.

**Proposition 18 (Hierarchical Boundary Conditions vs. Predetermination):**
Genetics, environment, and physics operate as nested boundary conditions on neural dynamics at distinct spatiotemporal scales: physics at $\sim 10^{-15}$s and below, biochemistry at $\sim 10^{-9}$s, neural dynamics at $\sim 10^{-3}$s, and cognitive architectures at $\sim 10^{0}$s. Boundary conditions constrain phase space without determining specific trajectories. Confusing constraint with predetermination is a fallacy of modal confusion: constraints on a system's degrees of freedom are not equivalent to a pre-written future state.

**Proposition 19 (Predictability ≠ Determinism ≠ Absence of Agency):**
The impossibility of predicting human choices from external observation (due to chaotic dynamics, quantum indeterminacy, and theory-of-mind opacity) does not imply the choices are uncaused or "free" in the libertarian sense. The mapping from causes to choices is deterministic (or quantum-random) at the micro-scale and unpredictable (chaotic) at the macro-scale, but agency is an emergent pattern at the cognitive scale—neither destroyed by determinism nor created by indeterminacy.

- **Proposition 20 (Causal Exclusion Dilemma of Reductive Compatibilism):**  
  Any framework that simultaneously asserts micro-causal closure and the causal efficacy of macroscopic reasons ($D = f(R)$) commits Kim’s causal exclusion fallacy. If micro-physical dynamics are causally sufficient, semantic evaluations and reasons are strictly epiphenomenal. Genuine agency requires information-theoretic causal emergence wherein the macroscopic organizational level exhibits strictly greater causal power than the microscopic substrate ($EI(\text{Macro}) > EI(\text{Micro})$), thereby falsifying micro-causal closure as an exhaustive ontology of causation.

- **Proposition 21 (Agency as Endogenous Constraint Selection):**  
  "Free will" is neither uncaused libertarian dualism nor passive algorithmic determinism; it is the ontological capacity of an autopoietic system characterized by organizational constraint closure to generate and select its own boundary conditions. Through non-holonomic macroscopic constraints, the organism harnesses microscopic thermodynamic degeneracy, canalizing micro-trajectories toward normative, teleonomically evaluated attractors.

- **Proposition 22 (Self-Referential Indeterminability of Deliberation):**  
  By Wolpert’s limits of physical inference and the non-ergodicity of recursive cognitive phase spaces, an embedded agent’s deliberate choice is formally incomputable and undetermined prior to its execution by any physical device sharing the agent’s causal past. Deliberation is an intrinsically participatory, symmetry-breaking act of self-determination rather than the coarse-grained observation of a predetermined state.
